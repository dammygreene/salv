# CULLER Phase 4 — Economic simulation report

Deterministic, pure-function simulations of the points -> reward-pool formula (`walletPoints / totalValidPoints * rewardPool`) across population sizes and participation patterns. **Purpose: understand emission behavior, not optimize toward a desired price.** No real $CULLER exists; `rewardPool` here is a fixed hypothetical value (1,000,000) used only so relative shares and concentration are comparable across scenarios.

Generated by `scripts/economic-simulations.ts`. Reward pool used: 1,000,000.

## Observations

- **Total emissions never exceed the reward pool** in any run above (enforced both by `simulateEpochRewards`'s remainder-absorption math and by the assertion this script runs after every scenario).
- **Concentration is a function of behavior, not population size.** LOW/NORMAL/HIGH participation stay broadly distributed (low top-wallet/top-10 share) regardless of whether there are 100 or 100,000 wallets, because every wallet follows the same random activity distribution — scale alone doesn't fix or worsen fairness.
- **A single heavy farmer can dominate a pool** under the current flat, unlimited points-per-action model: one wallet with thousands of its own empty accounts captures the large majority of the pool in `ONE_HEAVY_FARMER`, at every population size. This is a real, expected consequence of 'no reputation/streak scoring, pure proportional points' (explicitly required for Phase 3) combined with no per-wallet-per-epoch cap (not yet built) — worth deciding on deliberately, not something this simulation tries to hide or fix.
- **A sybil cluster's combined share is non-trivial even though each wallet looks like an ordinary light user.** In `MANY_SYBIL_WALLETS`, 20% of wallets (all 1-action, indistinguishable individually from genuine light users) collectively claim a meaningful slice of the pool purely through numbers — this is the quantitative case for the anti-farming work already flagged as architecture-only in Phase 3 (cross-wallet clustering, self-created-asset checks).
- **Mixed realistic adversarial conditions compound.** `MIXED_LEGIT_AND_FARMING` shows farmer and sybil shares both eating into the pool simultaneously, which is the more plausible real-world risk shape than either extreme alone.
